IELTS Overview

Complete exam guide — format, scoring, and everything you need to know

What is IELTS?

The International English Language Testing System (IELTS) is the world's most popular English proficiency test for higher education and global migration. Over 3.5 million tests are taken annually across 140+ countries, accepted by 11,000+ organisations worldwide.

Two versions: Academic (university admissions) and General Training (work/migration). Listening and Speaking are identical; Reading and Writing differ.

Total: 2h 45min. Results in 13 days.

FAQ

Can I use a pen for Listening? ▼
No, pencil is required for Listening and Reading. Pen is allowed for Writing.
How long is my score valid? ▼
IELTS scores are valid for 2 years from the test date.
Can I reschedule? ▼
Yes, up to 5 weeks before the test. Late cancellation may incur fees.
Examiner Tip

Most universities require 6.5 overall with no sub-score below 6.0. Top universities need 7.5+. Plan your target and focus on your weakest skill.

Listening

4 sections, 40 questions, 30 min + 10 min transfer

Section 1: Social Conversation

Two speakers in everyday situation (hotel booking, event registration). Form completion, table completion, MCQ.

Section 2: Social Monologue

Single speaker (tour guide, radio broadcast). Map labelling, matching, table completion.

Section 3: Educational Conversation

2-4 speakers in academic setting (tutorial, project discussion). MCQ, matching, sentence completion.

Section 4: Academic Lecture

Single speaker delivering a lecture. Note completion, summary completion.

10 Question Types

Form completionTable completionNote completionSentence completionSummary completionMCQMap labellingMatchingShort answerDiagram labelling

Key Strategies

  • Read ahead using breaks before each section
  • Underline keywords in questions before listening
  • Predict answer type (date, name, number, adjective)
  • Don't dwell on missed answers — move on
  • Check word limits: "NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS"
  • Spelling counts — wrong spelling = 0 marks

Reading

3 passages, 40 questions, 60 minutes, 13 question types

Strategies

Skim first (2 min): Read first sentence of each paragraph. Scan (1 min): Find keywords. Time: 20 min per passage exactly.

13 Question Types

TFNGYes/No/NGMatching headingsMatching infoMatching featuresMatching endingsMCQSentence completionSummary completionTable completionShort answerDiagram labellingFlow chart

Passage 1: Great Barrier Reef (TFNG)

Passage: The reef stretches 2,300 km along Australia's northeast coast. It has 2,900 reefs, 900 islands, 1,500 fish species. Visible from space. Generates $6B/year tourism. Bleaching events in 1998, 2002, 2016, 2017, 2020, 2022. Government invested $3B since 2015.

1. The reef is off Western Australia's coast.

2. The reef contains over 2,900 reef systems.

3. The government spent over $5B on conservation.

4. Bleaching occurred in 2016 and 2017.

Score: 0/4

Passage 2: The Science of Memory (TFNG + Sentence Completion)

Passage: Memory is not a single process but involves three stages: encoding, storage, and retrieval. Encoding converts sensory input into a mental representation. Storage maintains information over time, while retrieval accesses stored information when needed. There are two main types of memory: short-term memory, which holds about 7 items for 15-30 seconds, and long-term memory, which can store unlimited information indefinitely. Sleep plays a crucial role in memory consolidation. During deep sleep, the brain transfers information from short-term to long-term storage. Studies show that students who sleep after studying retain 40% more information than those who stay awake. Emotional events are remembered more vividly because the amygdala enhances memory consolidation during emotional arousal.

Questions 5-8: TFNG

5. Memory involves encoding, storage, and retrieval.

6. Short-term memory holds approximately 15 items.

7. Students retain 40% more after sleeping.

8. The amygdala enhances emotional memory.

Questions 9-13: Complete sentences (ONE WORD)

9. The three stages of memory are encoding, storage, and .

10. Short-term memory holds about items.

11. During deep sleep, the brain transfers memories to storage.

Score: 0/9

Examiner Tip

False = passage directly contradicts. Not Given = neither confirmed nor denied. If you can't find evidence in 2 minutes, mark NG and move on. For matching headings, read the first and last sentence of each paragraph first.

Common Mistakes
  • Over 20 min on one passage
  • Confusing False with Not Given
  • No skimming before scanning
  • Exceeding word limits

Writing

60 min - Task 1 (150 words) + Task 2 (250 words)

Academic Task 1

Describe visual data: line graph, bar chart, pie chart, table, process diagram, or map. At least 150 words.

Structure: 1. Paraphrase question 2. Overview (key trend) 3-4. Detailed paragraphs with data.

Key Vocabulary

rose steadilydeclined sharplyremained stablepeaked atfluctuatedupward trendaccounted forcompared towhereassimilarly

Sample: Line Graph

"The line graph shows international tourist numbers to three Southeast Asian countries from 2010 to 2020. Overall, all three saw an upward trend, with Thailand consistently highest. Thailand rose from 15M to a peak of 38M in 2019 before falling to 35M in 2020. Vietnam tripled from 5M to 15M, while Indonesia grew gradually from 7M to 12M."

General Training Task 1: Letter

Write a letter (formal, semi-formal, or informal). Types: complaint, request, invitation, apology, application, suggestion.

Structure: Salutation, reason for writing, bullet points, appropriate closing.

Tone Guide

Formal: Dear Sir/Madam, Yours faithfully. No contractions.
Semi-formal: Dear Mr Smith, Kind regards.
Informal: Dear John, Best wishes. Contractions OK.

Sample: Formal Complaint

"Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with a laptop purchased from your store on 12th January. The screen flickers intermittently, making it unusable. Despite contacting support twice, the issue remains. I request a replacement or full refund. Yours faithfully, James Mitchell"

Task 2: Essay

5 types: Opinion, Discussion, Problem/Solution, Advantages/Disadvantages, Two-part question. At least 250 words.

Structure: Intro (40-50 words), Body 1 (90-100), Body 2 (90-100), Conclusion (30-40).

Speaking

11-14 min, 3 parts, face-to-face with certified examiner

Part 1

Introduction & Interview

4-5 min. General questions on work/study, hometown, hobbies, family, food, travel.

Tip: Never answer yes/no. Extend with reason + example.

Part 2

Individual Long Turn

3-4 min. Cue card with 1 min prep + 2 min speaking. Use PPP structure: Past, Present, Perspective.

Part 3

Two-Way Discussion

4-5 min. Abstract questions on Part 2 topic. Use discourse markers: on the one hand, nevertheless, from my perspective.

Pronunciation Tips

  • Word stress: REcord vs reCORD
  • Sentence stress: content words carry meaning
  • TH sounds: voiced in "the", unvoiced in "think"
  • Vowel length: "ship" vs "sheep"
  • Weak forms: "I can [kən] swim"
  • Connected speech: "going to" to "gonna"
Examiner Tip

In Part 2, use the PPP technique: Past experience, Present details, Personal Perspective. This fills 2 minutes naturally.

Common Mistakes
  • One-word answers in Part 1
  • Stopping before 2 min in Part 2
  • Memorised responses
  • Going off-topic in Part 3

3-Month Timeline

Month 1

Weeks 1-2: Foundation

Take diagnostic test. Learn format. Start vocabulary (Environment, Health). Grammar review: Present/Past tenses. Listening Section 1-2. Skimming and scanning techniques.

Month 1

Weeks 3-4: Building

Writing Task 1 structure. Speaking Part 1 practice. Listening Sections 3-4. Reading: TFNG and headings. Vocabulary: Technology, Education. Start error log.

Month 2

Weeks 5-6: Intermediate

Writing Task 2 all types. Speaking Part 2 cue card practice. Full Listening tests. All reading question types. Vocabulary: Crime, Work. Grammar: Conditionals, Passive.

Month 2

Weeks 7-8: Advanced

Timed writing. Speaking Part 3 discussions. Full mock tests weekly. Deep error analysis. Vocabulary: Society, Media. Pronunciation focus.

Month 3

Weeks 9-10: Simulation

Full mock tests (2/week). Band tracking. Target weak areas. Refine essay structures. Record and self-evaluate speaking.

Month 3

Weeks 11-12: Final

Review strategies. Final mock test. Rest and confidence. Exam day prep. Light revision only.
